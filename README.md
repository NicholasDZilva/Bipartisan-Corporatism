# Bipartisan Corporatism: A Structural Interpretation of Policy Convergence

**Author:** Nicholas D'Zilva  
**Type:** Conceptual / Theoretical Framework  
**Status:** Published (SSRN)  

---

## Overview

This repository contains the paper:

**“Bipartisan Corporatism: A Structural Interpretation of Policy Convergence”**

The paper presents a structural model explaining how policy convergence can occur across competing political parties within democratic systems.

It introduces the concept of **bipartisan corporatism**, where electoral competition remains active, but policy formation converges around frameworks compatible with large, organised economic interests.

---

## Core Idea

The framework does **not** rely on:
- corruption
- conspiracy
- identical political parties

Instead, it explains convergence as an **emergent property** of:
- institutional constraints
- policy formation processes
- incentive structures within democratic systems

---

## Key Concepts

### 1. Bipartisan Corporatism
A condition where:
- political parties compete electorally
- but policy formation converges structurally

---

### 2. Policy Filter
A structural bias in policy formation:

- Policies that **create markets or expand economic activity**  
  → more likely to gain traction  

- Policies that **impose liability or restrict profitable industries**  
  → less likely to scale into full policy frameworks  

---

### 3. Justification Lock
A stabilising mechanism:

- Policy decisions are repeatedly justified using the same logic  
- Over time, these justifications become **default assumptions**  
- Alternatives face increasing burden of proof  

---

### 4. Constrained Policy Space

The framework distinguishes between:
- **Visible political contestation**  
- **Underlying policy boundaries**

Policy debate is real—but it occurs within a **pre-structured range of viable options**.

---

## What the Paper Explains

- Why policy convergence occurs across competing parties  
- Why policy variation tends to be bounded  
- Why structural divergence is rare and often temporary  
- How institutional incentives shape policy viability  

---

## What the Paper Does NOT Claim

- That all policy is captured  
- That political competition is fake  
- That outcomes are coordinated or conspiratorial  

The framework is **structural**, not accusatory.

---

## Boundary Conditions

The model allows for:
- crisis-driven policy shifts  
- temporary divergence  

However, these tend to be:
- absorbed into existing institutional structures  
- stabilised over time
